Old tax regime slabs for tax year 2026-27 (FY 2026-27)

The old regime has higher rates than the new one but lets you subtract many deductions and exemptions first. Its slabs depend on age, and they are unchanged for income earned from 1 April 2026 to 31 March 2027.

The age is counted at any time during the year: a person who turns 60 on any day of the year uses the senior citizen slabs for the whole year.

What goes with the slabs

  • Standard deduction: ₹50,000 for salaried people and pensioners.
  • Rebate: a resident individual with taxable income up to ₹5,00,000 gets a rebate of up to ₹12,500, which brings the tax to nil.
  • Cess: 4% health and education cess on the tax. Surcharge above ₹50 lakh.

A worked example

Salary ₹12,00,000, age below 60, deductions of ₹2,00,000 besides the standard deduction. Taxable income: ₹12,00,000 minus ₹50,000 minus ₹2,00,000 is ₹9,50,000. The tax:

  1. Nil on ₹2,50,000: ₹0
  2. 5% on ₹2,50,000: ₹12,500
  3. 20% on ₹4,50,000: ₹90,000

That adds up to ₹1,02,500. With 4% cess the tax for the year is ₹1,06,600. The same salary in the new regime, with no deductions, is taxed at ₹0.

Common questions

Who can use the old regime?

Anyone. The new regime is the default, but an individual can opt for the old regime. People without business income can choose each year in the return, filed by the due date.

Up to what income is there no tax in the old regime?

A resident individual with taxable income up to ₹5,00,000 pays no tax because of the rebate of up to ₹12,500. The basic exemption itself is ₹2,50,000 below 60, ₹3,00,000 from 60 to 79 and ₹5,00,000 from 80.

Is the standard deduction different in the old regime?

Yes. It is ₹50,000 in the old regime and ₹75,000 in the new regime.
